# Helpfulness rubric (1-5)

You are grading a customer-support reply. Judge whether the reply resolves the customer's
question using only the supplied policy context. Ignore tone, formatting, length, and
personality unless they change whether the customer can act on the answer.

## Score 5 — Excellent

The reply answers the exact question, states the applicable policy correctly, and gives the
customer a clear next action. No relevant policy detail is missing and nothing is invented.

## Score 4 — Good

The reply answers the question and is actionable, but omits one minor relevant detail (for
example the exact refund window) or is slightly less direct than it could be.

## Score 3 — Adequate

The reply is broadly correct and relevant, but the customer would still need to ask a
follow-up question to act. No policy is contradicted.

## Score 2 — Weak

The reply addresses the topic but misses the key policy fact, gives a vague next step, or
includes a minor unsupported claim. The customer cannot confidently act on it.

## Score 1 — Failure

The reply does not answer the question, contradicts the policy, invents a policy or timeline,
asks for a password or full card number, or is a generic non-answer. This is an explicit
failure.

## How to score

- Read the customer message, the policy context, the expected facts, and the candidate reply.
- Check correctness first, then completeness, then actionability.
- Do not reward confident wording. Do not penalise plain wording.
- If the policy context does not cover the question, a reply that says so and offers to check
  can score 4 or 5; a reply that invents an answer cannot score above 2.
